How to calculate a joint probability

Often, a scientist will need to know the probability that multiple events will occur at the same time, which is called a joint probability. If these events stem from independent processes, we can multiply the probabilities of these independent events to find the joint probability.

In this training, you will learn how to calculate the probability that two or more independent events will occur together.

Instructions

To learn how to calculate a joint probability, we will use the following example.

Imagine that your university has a baseball team and a football team, both of which are playing next Saturday. You want to calculate the probability that both teams will win. Because each team competes in its own league, the probability of either team winning does not depend on the probability that the other team will win. In other words, these probabilities are independent of each other.

The probability that both teams will win on the same day is called a joint probability, because this probability joins two independent probabilities in one calculation.

Step 1: Identify the two independent events

In our example, the two independent events are as follows:

  1. the probability that a specific baseball team wins, and
  2. the probability that a specific football team wins.

Step 2: Identify the probability of each event

To calculate a joint probability, you must know the probability that each event will occur. For our example, assume that you have already used data from past games to estimate the probability that each team will win. The probability that the baseball team will win its game equals 0.67 (67%). However, the probability that the football team will win its game equals only 0.04 (4%).

Step 3: Multiply the independent probabilities to get the joint probability

To calculate a joint probability, you need to use the product rule of probability:

The probability of two independent events occurring together, P(A and B), equals the product of their independent probabilities, P(A) ∙ P(B).

In our example, we should calculate the joint probability by multiplying the probability that the baseball team will win, P(A), by the probability that the football team wins, P(B):

P(A and B) = P(A) ∙ P(B)

P(A and B) = 0.67 ∙ 0.04

P(A and B) = 0.03

Therefore, the probability that both teams will win is 0.03, or 3%. Pretty bad odds!

Practice Problem

An engineer wants to know the probability that a machine will function at a high temperature and a high relative humidity. Temperature and humidity affect the function of this machine independently:

  1. The probability that the machine works at a high temperature equals 0.92.
  2. The probability that the machine works at a high relative humidity equals 0.80.

What is the probability that the machine will function at both a high temperature and a high relative humidity?

Correct Answer:

P(A and B) = 0.92 ∙ 0.80 = 0.736

The joint probability equals 0.736, or 73.6%. This value represents the probability that the machine will function when both independent conditions occur—high temperature and high relative humidity.

Summary

  • A joint probability is the product of two or more independent probabilities
  • By multiplying the probabilities of independent events, you can calculate the probability that all of these events will occur
  • Joint probabilities are important for predicting how multiple processes will combine to produce an outcome
